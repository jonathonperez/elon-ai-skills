# Patterns & Techniques

## The Algorithm (five steps, strict order)
**When to use**: designing or fixing any product, process, or system.
**How**: 1) Question every requirement (named owner required) → 2) Delete parts/processes (re-add ~10% later or you under-deleted) → 3) Simplify what survives → 4) Accelerate cycle time → 5) Automate.
**Trade-offs**: deliberate over-deletion causes some rework; that's the design. Running steps out of order automates/optimizes waste.

## Idiot-Index Audit
**When to use**: attacking cost anywhere.
**How**: for each part, compute finished cost ÷ raw-material cost. Rank. Attack the highest ratios with better manufacturing or in-housing. Require owners to know their top/bottom five by heart.
**Trade-offs**: ignores R&D/tooling amortization on tiny volumes; strongest for parts made at scale.

## Cross-Industry Price Benchmark
**When to use**: a quoted component price feels high.
**How**: find the cheapest industry solving the same function (bathroom latch → rocket latch, house AC → rocket cooling); adapt the commodity part or build in-house.
**Trade-offs**: requires validation the cheaper part meets the true (post-Algorithm-step-1) requirements.

## Find the Limit
**When to use**: deciding how far to cut mass, thickness, cost, schedule.
**How**: ask the people doing the work what's safe, then test one notch beyond their comfort (4.8mm → "what about four?"). Keep the result if reality accepts it.
**Trade-offs**: consumes test articles and occasional failures — priced in ("if we're not occasionally blowing up an engine, we're not trying hard enough").

## 24-Hour Cadence on the Critical Problem
**When to use**: something urgent is broken or a deadline is existential.
**How**: meet daily (or nightly) with the actual doers; run the Algorithm each session; check the previous day's progress; drop frequency the moment urgency resolves.
**Trade-offs**: burns leadership attention — reserve for the genuine bottleneck.

## Walk to the Red
**When to use**: managing any pipeline or production flow.
**How**: instrument every station green/red; go physically to red stations; interrogate to root cause (who owns it, why does it exist, can we delete it); make ~100 small command decisions, accept 20% wrong.
**Trade-offs**: needs live instrumentation and tolerance for reversed decisions.

## Go to the Problem / Talent Air-Drop
**When to use**: a remote unit is failing or slow.
**How**: fly to the source immediately, stay until fixed; or drop a small squad of trusted problem-solvers from another part of the empire into the failing unit (they need general engineering excellence, not domain experience).
**Trade-offs**: disruptive by design; morale cost in the receiving unit.

## One Dramatic Demonstration
**When to use**: raising money, closing a partner, launching a product.
**How**: build a working version of the thing before the meeting (Daimler electric smart car); let them use it; ask for the deal while they're stunned.
**Trade-offs**: demo scramble diverts the team; a failed live demo is worse than slides (Page/Brin 10-mph ride survived on goodwill).

## First-Principles Cost Deconstruction
**When to use**: "it's always cost $X" claims.
**How**: list the raw materials → price them on open markets → the gap between that and quoted price is your addressable opportunity.
**Trade-offs**: manufacturing cleverness to close the gap is the actual hard work.

## Silent-Thinking Session
**When to use**: a hard problem resists solution in normal work mode.
**How**: lie flat, dark, earplugs + sleep mask, no stimuli, 20–60+ minutes; don't steer thoughts; capture what surfaces.
**Trade-offs**: none material; requires actually protecting the time.

## Skip-Level Problem Solving
**When to use**: whenever there are problems to solve.
**How**: meet the level below your managers — the people doing the actual work; ask them what's safe, what's slow, what's stupid.
**Trade-offs**: managers may feel undermined; Elon accepts that as the price of truth.

## Mission Framing (Epoch Significance)
**When to use**: motivating a team through brutal timelines.
**How**: connect today's task to the largest honest consequence (reusability → multiplanetary civilization; milliseconds/step → millions of units). Repeat it constantly — "repetition is persuasive."
**Trade-offs**: only works if you visibly believe it and share the pain (frontline general).

## Bridge Preservation
**When to use**: being wronged by partners, boards, co-founders.
**How**: absorb the loss without burning the relationship; the network pays back later (PayPal coup → Founders Fund saving SpaceX).
**Trade-offs**: swallowing pride now for optionality later.
