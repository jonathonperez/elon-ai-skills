## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The Algorithm** (the single most important idea — five steps, strict order):
1. **Question every requirement** — each needs a *named human owner*, never a department; question it even if it came from Elon. Requirements from smart people are most dangerous because nobody questions them.
2. **Delete** any part or process you can — if you don't re-add ≥10% later, you didn't delete enough.
3. **Simplify** — only after deleting; never optimize what shouldn't exist.
4. **Accelerate** cycle time — only after steps 1–3.
5. **Automate** — last, always. Tesla's near-fatal mistake was doing this first.

**First-principles thinking**: reason up from physics and raw-material truth, not by analogy to precedent. Battery packs "cost $600/kWh forever" → materials on the London Metal Exchange cost $80/kWh → the gap is manufacturing, attack it. "Physics is law; everything else is a recommendation."

**The Idiot Index**: finished-product cost ÷ raw-material cost. High ratio = attackable waste. Every owner must know their best/worst parts by this metric cold.

**A maniacal sense of urgency is our operating principle**: speed is offense AND defense (the never-hit SR-71 had no defense but acceleration). Time is the only currency — okay to scrap money or equipment, never time. Meet every 24h on existential problems. Decide fast, accept ~20% wrong: "If I don't make decisions, we die."

**Hand-on-the-stove org design**: never separate design, engineering, and manufacturing — the decision-maker must feel the pain of their decision immediately. Engineers sit at the assembly line; managers do the real work ≥20% of time ("a cavalry leader who can't ride a horse"); communication takes the shortest path, never the chain of command.

**Frontline general**: lead from where the work is. Fly to the problem, live in the factory, sleep on the floor. "Nobody bleeds for the prince in the palace. Wherever Napoleon was, that's where his armies would do best."

**Talent = the whole game**: "The output of any company is the vector sum of the people within it." Small elite teams beat large mediocre ones. Hire attitude over skill ("attitude change requires a brain transplant"). Camaraderie is dangerous — you are the judge, not the friend; sparing one B-player hurts everyone depending on the mission.

**Mission-first, backfill economics**: start with what must exist ("wouldn't happen without us"), frame it at epoch significance ("multiplanetary civilization"), and let belief transfer to investors and teams. Showmanship is salesmanship: one dramatic demonstration (the working electric smart car that won Daimler's $50M) beats any deck.

**Cost control compounds**: costs, unlike profits, are permanent savings (Carnegie's mantra). Benchmark parts against cheaper industries ($1,500 NASA latch → $30 bathroom-stall latch). Vertically integrate because the supply chain's speed becomes your speed.

**Truth over wishful thinking**: "I always assume we're losing even when it looks like we might win." Bad news loudly and often; good news quietly and once. Okay to be wrong — never confident and wrong.

**Pain is the price**: "Excellence is the capacity to take pain." Never give up ("I'd have to be dead or completely incapacitated"). Fear is normal; look at it directly and let mission importance drive you. Losses stop hurting after ~50 — then you take better risks.
